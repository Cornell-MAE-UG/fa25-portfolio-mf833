We chose to built this Testbox for Combat Robotics @ Cornell because it is one of the most critical safety tools on our project team. Our robots can spin weapons at nearly 200 mph, so we needed a secure enclosure where we could safely verify motor directions, run initial spin-ups, and test drivetrain behavior without risking injuries or uncontrolled failures. 
We started by cutting and measuring all the wooden panels, ensuring every part fit tightly within the frame’s design envelope. Each wooden piece was individually sanded to achieve clean edges, consistent thickness, and proper alignment during assembly.

![Shaded rendering of earlier version]({{ "/assets/images/testboxpowertool.jpg" | relative_url }}){: .inline-image-r style="width: 200px"}

Once the wooden components were prepared, I moved on to fabricating the supporting metal structure. I cut the steel pieces to length, clamped them in position, and welded the frame together while checking squareness and alignment at every stage. 
The welded frame provides the rigidity needed to contain high-energy impacts and withstand the vibrations produced during weapon spin-up testing. After welding, I test-fit the wood panels against the steel frame to verify tolerances, spacing, and overall geometry before committing to final attachment.

![Photo of old radio]({{ "/assets/images/testboxunwelded.jpg" | relative_url }}){: .inline-image-l style="width: 200px"}

To do the floor of the Testbox, we constructed and painted a separate wooden floor by individually cutting, sanding, and sizing each plank for a tight triangular fit. We installed the panels onto the welded frame and fully assembled the lid for easy access inside the testbox.

![Photo of old radio]({{ "/assets/images/testboxwelding.jpg" | relative_url }}){: .inline-image-l style="width: 200px"}

This project strengthened my hands-on fabrication abilities: woodworking, welding, measurement precision, and structural assembly. While also giving the team a safe, reliable system for testing our combat robots under real load conditions.
