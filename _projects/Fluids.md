In this project, I focused on the fluid-mechanical dissection and mathematical modeling of a Campman propane stove burner. I analyzed the internal components in detail, breaking down how the valve, metal housing, and nozzle features restrict and redirect the flow as propane transitions from the tank to the burner. Using these physical insights, I helped develop and expand the mathematical model of the system, treating the screws and valve as flow-limiting nozzles in series and solving for the resulting mass flow rate and exit velocity. I also contributed to the analysis of the burner’s airfoils by applying conservation principles to explain how the geometry influences mixing and deceleration of the propane–air mixture. My work tied the hardware dissection to the governing equations, allowing the team to quantify how component design controls mass flow, pressure drop, and overall burner performance.
