My team analyzed a single-axis spacecraft attitude control system (ACS) to understand how sensing, actuation, and control laws work together to achieve precision pointing. My role focused on deriving and interpreting the simplified rotational dynamics model using ODEs and linearization, leading to our governing equation. As a group, we evaluated the full closed-loop block diagram, examined the PID control structure, and assessed key performance metrics such as settling time, steady-state error, and overall system stability. Our work framed the ACS as it actually functions in spacecraft: a feedback system that continually measures orientation, processes error, and commands a reaction wheel to correct it.

We also analyzed major environmental disturbances such as solar radiation pressure and atmospheric drag, and quantified how each produces torque based on satellite geometry, surface interactions, and offsets between the center of pressure and center of mass. By integrating these disturbances conceptually into the control model, we evaluated their impact on pointing performance and disturbance rejection. Finally, we studied pointing-accuracy requirements using RMS error to understand how real missions define allowable angular deviation. Overall, the project demonstrates how core System Dynamics concepts (ODEs, transfer functions, block diagrams, performance metrics, and feedback control) explain the behavior and limitations of an actual spacecraft attitude-control system.
